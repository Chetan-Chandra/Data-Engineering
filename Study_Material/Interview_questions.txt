# Resume-Based Interview Question Bank — BI Developer / Data Engineer

Your resume positions you as a professional with 10 years of experience, moving from database development and BI into cloud data engineering and platform modernization. Interviewers will likely test whether you can explain the technologies listed, defend your project contributions, solve production scenarios, and make design decisions—not just define terminology.

I'll organize this around your actual resume, starting with the questions most likely to expose gaps. This is a comprehensive question bank, not a claim that every interviewer will ask every question.

Important: Your resume uses strong phrases such as “architected end-to-end,” “led the migration,” “boost query performance,” “zero post-launch downtime,” and “8% increase in profitability.” Be prepared to explain your specific contribution, implementation, evidence, and results for each. Don't claim ownership of work you only supported.

## 18. Scenario-based questions

These questions test how you reason through real production problems. You may not have encountered every scenario before; explain your investigation and decision-making process.

Scenario 1

Your pipeline loaded 1 million rows, but the source has 1.05 million. What do you do?

Be ready to discuss extraction windows, filters, pagination, watermark logic, rejected rows, count reconciliation and safe reprocessing.

Scenario 2

A Snowflake bill suddenly doubles. How do you investigate?

Discuss warehouse usage, query history, query profiles, warehouse sizing, concurrency, unnecessary full refreshes and cost controls.

Scenario 3

A source system adds a column and a downstream dashboard breaks. What do you do?

Cover schema-change detection, compatibility, dbt tests, model contracts where applicable, impact analysis and controlled deployment.

Scenario 4

An Airflow task fails after writing half the output. How do you recover?

Explain checkpoints, atomic publication, idempotency, temporary output locations and safe retries.

Scenario 5

The business reports that yesterday's revenue is incorrect.

Trace the metric from dashboard to model, source data and transformation logic. Check freshness, duplicate records, joins, late data and reconciliation.

Scenario 6

You must migrate 100 tables and 20 dashboards with no business interruption.

Discuss inventory, dependency mapping, migration waves, parallel runs, validation, cutover, monitoring and rollback.

Scenario 7

Your pipeline is reliable but too slow. How do you improve it?

First identify the bottleneck, then consider source extraction, file sizes, parallelism, network transfer, warehouse compute and transformation design.

Scenario 8

A production deployment breaks several downstream models.

Explain how you would stop further impact, assess dependencies, roll back or revert the change, recover data and introduce regression tests.

Additional scenarios to practise:

- A source system is unavailable during a scheduled load.
- A message is delivered twice by a streaming service.
- A late update changes a record that was already processed.
- A Terraform state file is unavailable or out of date.
- A query is fast in development but slow in production.
- Two dashboards show different values for the same KPI.
- A stakeholder requests access to sensitive data.
- A data pipeline must recover after a regional outage.
- An upstream source changes its timestamp format.
- A data-quality test fails after the data has already been loaded.
- A pipeline's backlog grows faster than the consumer can process it.
- A migration passes technical tests but business users reject the results.
